move C — arcs, circles, and three-point primitives

The third trajectory primitive every controller ships. Specify start, intermediate, and goal poses; the controller fits an arc through them and follows it.

Why arcs

Welding curved seams, tracing the edge of a circular flange, polishing a curved surface. None of these can be done with move L without breaking the path into hundreds of micro-segments. move C lets you specify "go around this arc" with three points and let the controller handle the rest.

Many tasks naturally trace circular arcs — drawing a curved seam, polishing a curved edge, machining a fillet. Approximating them with many small move L segments works but produces a faceted path. move C produces a true smooth arc.

Three-point arc construction

Given three positions p0, pm, p1 (start, midpoint, end), there is a unique circle passing through them — the circumscribed circle of the triangle. The arc from p0 through pm to p1 is the relevant portion.

To follow the arc, parameterise by angle θ from the centre: x(θ) = c + r·(cos(θ)·u + sin(θ)·v), where u and v are orthogonal unit vectors in the plane of the circle and θ ranges from θ0 to θ1.

What about orientation?

Position alone isn't enough. The end-effector also has an orientation that we usually want either fixed (welding torch always vertical) or smoothly interpolated along the arc (tool-tip aligned with arc tangent). The choice is task-specific. The controller typically offers both modes.

Quick recap

  • move C = arc through three points (start, midpoint, goal).
  • Parameterise by angle along the arc; solve IK at each waypoint with warm-start.
  • Orientation along the arc is a separate design choice — typically fixed-world or tangent-following.
