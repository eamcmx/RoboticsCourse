Lecture 08 · Module 2

SCARA: Adept Cobra S800

A real industrial four-axis arm. Two horizontal revolute joints reach in the plane; a vertical slide for z; a final wrist rotation for tool orientation. The geometry you'll see in every modern pick-and-place line.

A real-world SCARA

We have one of these on the lab bench. The geometry is the same as on every Adept Cobra — and very similar to other SCARA robots from Epson, Yaskawa, ABB. If you understand this one robot you can read the manuals of any of them.

SCARA stands for Selective Compliance Articulated Robot Arm. The compliance is selective because the arm is rigid in the vertical direction (the prismatic axis) and compliant in the horizontal plane (the two revolute joints can absorb side-loads via the encoders). This is the optimal geometry for vertical insertion tasks — placing a chip on a board, pressing a button, dropping a screw.

Play with it

Four sliders: shoulder θ1, elbow θ2, vertical slide d3, tool rotation θ4. The reach is 800 mm — switch q3 by 0.1 m to see the vertical travel.

Reach and reachability

With shoulder length L₁ = 0.425 m and elbow length L₂ = 0.375 m, the planar reach varies between L₁ + L₂ = 0.800 m (arm fully extended) and |L₁ − L₂| = 0.050 m (arm folded). Anywhere in that annular region of the x–y plane is reachable.

Vertical travel adds the third dimension: z spans (d₁ + q₃min) to (d₁ + q₃max). The full workspace is therefore an annular cylinder.

Quick recap

  • SCARA = two horizontal revolute + one vertical prismatic + one tool rotation. Standard pick-and-place geometry.
  • Workspace is an annular cylinder. Inner radius = |L₁−L₂|; outer = L₁+L₂.
  • Closed-form IK exists for the 2-link planar shoulder/elbow part — the wrist and slide are decoupled.
Homework 08 · due before next session

Drive the SCARA from a target pose

  1. By hand: implement closed-form inverse kinematics for the SCARA shoulder/elbow given a target (x, y). Both elbow-up and elbow-down solutions.
  2. In code: write ik_cobra(p, theta4) that takes a target position and a desired tool angle, returns four joint values.
  3. Plot the reachable workspace by sampling 1000 random joint vectors uniformly in the joint limits and scatter-plotting the end-effector positions. Annotate the inner and outer radii.
