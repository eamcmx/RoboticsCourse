Lecture 19 · Module 4

Trajectories on a moving plate — chained transforms

When the workpiece itself is being held by another robot, the trajectory is defined in a frame that's no longer fixed. Every via-point has to be re-expressed in the world before IK can run.

Two robots, one trajectory

Imagine robot A holding a plate. Robot B is going to draw a line on the plate. If the plate were sitting on a table the trajectory would just be a list of poses in the world. But the plate is moving — robot A is rotating it. So every pose has to be defined in the plate's frame, then transformed into the world via robot A's end-effector pose.

The setup: robot A holds a flat workpiece (a "plate") rigidly at its end-effector. The trajectory we want robot B to follow is defined relative to the plate's surface — say, the four corners of a rectangle drawn on it. As long as A is still, the rectangle is just four world-frame poses and B can run move L between them.

The interesting case: A moves while B is working. Now the rectangle's world-frame coordinates change at every tick.

The transform chain

Let:

  • TA0(qA) be robot A's end-effector pose in the world (controlled by A's joints).
  • TplateA be the plate's pose relative to A's end-effector (constant — the plate is rigidly attached).
  • Tviaplate be the desired pose for robot B in the plate's frame (e.g., a corner of the rectangle).

Then the world-frame target for robot B is the chain

Run B's IK on TB0 at every tick. As A moves, the chain product evolves and B's targets shift; B's IK keeps tracking them.

Practical concerns

  • Sampling rate. The chain has to be re-evaluated at every controller tick — typically 1 kHz on industrial hardware, 50–100 Hz in simulation.
  • Latency. If reading TA0 from A is slow, B will lag. Match the loop rate to the sensor update.
  • Constraint compatibility. A's motion plus B's required pose may exceed B's reach at some plate positions. Plan for unreachable regions.

Quick recap

  • When the workpiece is on a moving robot, every via-point is the chain TA·TplateA·Tviaplate.
  • Re-evaluate the chain at every controller tick.
  • Plan around the joint A's motion — some plate positions push B's targets out of B's workspace.
Homework 19 · due before next session

Track a square on a rotating plate

  1. Define a 10×10 cm square in plate frame.
  2. Drive robot A through a 90° rotation around its z-axis over 100 ticks. At each tick, compute the world-frame poses of all four square corners.
  3. Run move L on robot B between successive corners. Plot B's end-effector path in the world frame and (separately) in the plate frame. The plate-frame path should be a perfect square; the world-frame path should be a sheared/curved version of it.
