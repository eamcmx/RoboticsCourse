Lecture 29 · Course wrap-up

Wrap-up — what we covered, where to go next

Twenty lectures, one continuous worked example, four robots, two simulators, one master–slave demo. A retrospective and a map of what comes after.

Looking back

When we started the term I asked you to remember that we were building one connected worked example. From frames of reference in Lecture 1 to a master–slave dual-robot system in Lecture 20. If you stop and look at it now — every concept along the way is still in use at the end. Nothing was wasted, nothing was thrown away.

The whole arc:

  • Module 1 (Lectures 1–4): Frames, rotations, translations, homogeneous transforms, inverses. The mathematical machine.
  • Module 2 (Lectures 5–10): Apply the machine to robots. Rigid bodies, prismatic and revolute joints, real arms, the DH convention.
  • Module 3 (Lectures 11–13): Inverse kinematics. The Jacobian, Newton–Raphson, joint limits, singularities.
  • Module 4 (Lectures 14–21): Trajectory planning and integration. move J / L / C, hardware interfaces, real-time control, the master–slave system.

What you can now do

Concretely:

  • Read a robot manual and produce a working forward-kinematics implementation from the DH table inside an hour.
  • Solve inverse kinematics numerically for any serial chain up to 12 DOF, with warm-starting and damping.
  • Plan trajectories using the three standard primitives (J, L, C) and time-parameterise them.
  • Wire a hardware teach-pendant to a real or simulated controller.
  • Compose multi-robot systems where one robot's state defines another's task.

That set of skills is the practical core of any factory automation engineering job.

Where to go next

Topics that build directly on this course:

  • Dynamics. So far q has been the variable. Force/torque is the next layer. Newton–Euler, Lagrangian formulation, mass matrices. Course companion: Spong, Hutchinson & Vidyasagar, "Robot Modeling and Control."
  • Control. Closing the loop on actual joint commands. PID per joint, computed-torque control, impedance/admittance control for contact tasks.
  • Motion planning. Beyond move L between fixed points: collision-free paths through obstacles, RRT and PRM algorithms, optimisation-based planning.
  • Computer vision for manipulation. The end-effector pose is no longer pre-specified; it's computed from camera input. Visual servoing.
  • Learning-based methods. Reinforcement learning, imitation learning, policies for grasping and manipulation. The current research frontier.

Recommended next reading

  • Spong, Hutchinson & Vidyasagar — Robot Modeling and Control. The standard graduate text.
  • Lynch & Park — Modern Robotics. Free PDF; especially good on twists/screws and computed torque.
  • Siciliano, Sciavicco, Villani & Oriolo — Robotics: Modelling, Planning and Control. Comprehensive, slightly more advanced.
  • Merchán-Cruz, Aragón-Camarasa & Gromova — IK_benchmark_2026. Direct extension of this course; comparison of 26 IK solvers across 4 robots and 4 trajectories. github.com/eamcmx/IK_benchmark_2026.

That's a non-trivial thing to know how to do. Stay in touch when you go on into industry; I'm curious which of these techniques you actually end up using.
